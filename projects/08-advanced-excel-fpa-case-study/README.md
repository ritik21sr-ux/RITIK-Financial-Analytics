# Advanced Excel FP&A Case Study — Department Spend Analysis

**Status: In progress — practice case study**  
**Owner:** Ritik Srivastava  
**Tools:** Microsoft Excel, SUMIFS, XLOOKUP, PivotTables, conditional formatting, charts

## Business scenario

You are supporting the FP&A team of a fictional multi-department business. Management wants to understand monthly actual spend against budget, identify overspending departments and categories, and receive practical recommendations.

## Data and integrity

- The workbook uses a synthetic dataset covering January–June 2026.
- It contains 180 fictional transaction-level records across five departments and six expense categories.
- No employer, client, confidential or personal financial data is used.
- This is an independent learning demonstration, not client work or an employer deliverable.

## Workbook

The companion practice workbook is being completed locally. It contains:
- Transaction-level actuals
- Monthly department budget
- An analysis worksheet with formula tasks
- Formula examples and interview preparation questions

## Planned analysis

1. Calculate monthly department budgets and actuals with `SUMIFS`.
2. Calculate INR variance and variance percentage.
3. Classify over-budget and within/below-budget results.
4. Build a PivotTable by department and month.
5. Drill down to category and vendor drivers.
6. Build an executive summary dashboard.
7. Write concise management commentary and recommendations.

## Planned deliverables

- Completed Excel analysis workbook
- PivotTable and dashboard screenshots
- Formula explanations
- A short written summary of the key findings
- A 90-second interview walkthrough

## Learning objectives

Demonstrate how an FP&A analyst structures a budget-versus-actual review, validates data, explains spending variances and communicates actions to management.

## Limitations

The dataset is intentionally small and simplified for practice. It does not represent a real company's chart of accounts, accounting policy, approval workflow or ERP extract. Findings must not be described as real business results.
